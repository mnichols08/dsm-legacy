Diamond Star Motors (DSM) was one of the most influential automotive collaborations in 1980s-1990s America, resulting in an enduring fanbase and vehicles that are now icons in car culture. This partnership was tightly intertwined with the history of the legendary Mitsubishi 4G63 engine, whose rally-bred credentials set it apart as one of the most beloved four-cylinders in the world. The following explains the complete story—covering the business, the cars, and the powerhouse engines that defined the DSM era and the rise of the 4G63.

### Origins: Mitsubishi and Chrysler Partnership

- In the early 1970s, Chrysler bought a 15% stake in Mitsubishi, allowing Chrysler to market rebadged Mitsubishis as Dodge, Plymouth, and Chrysler-branded models (like the Dodge Colt) in the U.S.[1][2][3]
- The partnership helped Chrysler fill the need for compact efficient vehicles during the oil crises, while Mitsubishi got access to the U.S. market.
- Voluntary import quotas in the early 1980s limited Japanese-branded sales, so both companies saw advantages in further integration.[3][1]

### Formation and Growth of Diamond Star Motors

- Facing legal and market obstacles, Chrysler and Mitsubishi formally created Diamond Star Motors in October 1985 to build vehicles in the U.S. and sidestep quotas.[4][2][1][3]
- A new plant was built in Normal, Illinois, opening in 1988 with a 240,000-vehicle annual capacity.
- The first vehicles—Plymouth Laser, Mitsubishi Eclipse, and Eagle Talon—used a shared platform with advanced features for their era, driving a new performance wave in the compact segment.[5][6][1]

### DSM Vehicles and Their Engines

- The key DSM cars (Eclipse, Talon, Laser) launched in 1990 used a range of engines:
   - Base versions received naturally aspirated SOHC or DOHC four-cylinders.
   - Higher trims, like the Eclipse GS Turbo and Talon TSi AWD, were powered by the Mitsubishi 4G63T—an intercooled, turbocharged 2.0L DOHC I4 renowned for its strength and modifiability.[7][8]
- Other cars, like the Mitsubishi Galant VR-4, Dodge Stealth, and Colt Turbo, showcased Mitsubishi's turbocharging and all-wheel-drive prowess in the '80s-'90s.[9][10][5]

### The Legendary 4G63 Engine

- The Mitsubishi Sirius-series engines (4G6x) started in the late 1970s, first designed as sturdy, efficient, and tunable platforms.[11][9]
- The turbocharged 4G63T debuted in the 1988 Galant VR-4, quickly gaining a reputation for reliability and immense potential.[12][8]
- Used in vehicles such as the:
   - Mitsubishi Lancer Evolution I-IX (and rally cars)
   - Mitsubishi Galant VR-4
   - DSM triplets (Eclipse/Talon/Laser GSX/GST/TSi)
   - Several other Japanese and world market models[8][12][11]
- Key features include a strong iron block, robust crank and rods, and impressive aftermarket support—many builds achieving 600+hp on stock internals with proper tuning.[13][11]
- The 4G63 powered numerous racing wins, especially in rally with the Lancer Evolution and Galant VR-4. Tommi Mäkinen notably drove 4G63-powered Evos to multiple World Rally Championship victories in the late 1990s.[12][8]

### Business End and Enduring Legacy

- In 1991, Chrysler, struggling financially, sold its stake, making DSM a Mitsubishi-owned operation (renamed Mitsubishi Motor Manufacturing of America in 1995).
- Mitsubishi continued the Eclipse line until the 2012 model year, but the peak of performance, innovation, and tunability remains firmly attached to the DSM era (1990-1999).[2][14]
- Today, DSMs and the 4G63 remain enthusiast favorites, supported by a vibrant aftermarket and substantial motorsport pedigree.[4][11][8]

### Predecessor Powerhouse Engines and Vehicles

- Before DSM and the 4G63, Mitsubishi developed:
  - The Sirius series, which included the Lancer EX 2000 Turbo—a 1981 sedan that foreshadowed the turbocharging trend and set the foundation for the 4G63T’s success.
  - The Starion, another turbocharged RWD sports car, showed Mitsubishi’s early turbo know-how.
  - Earlier Mitsubishi Galants, Mirages, and Lancers, which adopted technological upgrades like multi-valve heads and turbocharging leading up to the DSM era.[15][9]

***

Diamond Star Motors is remembered as a bridge between '80s Japanese performance engineering and the explosion of 1990s tuner culture, all anchored by the legendary 4G63 engine—a combination that left a permanent mark on the world of affordable performance cars.[1][2][11][8][4][12]
