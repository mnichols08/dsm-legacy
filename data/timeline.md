Here is a detailed timeline of the key corporate milestones, partnership changes, and ownership events in Diamond Star Motors (DSM) history, focused solely on the automotive DSM joint venture between Mitsubishi and Chrysler:

### Timeline of DSM Corporate Events

- **1970:** Chrysler acquires a 15% stake in Mitsubishi, beginning a partnership with vehicle imports under Chrysler brands in the United States.[1][2]
- **Early 1980s:** Increased conflict over import quotas and direct competition, with Mitsubishi wishing to sell through its own branded dealerships in the U.S..[2][1]
- **October 1985:** Diamond-Star Motors officially incorporated as a joint venture (50-50 split) between Chrysler and Mitsubishi, aiming to evade import quotas and strengthen both companies' U.S. market position.[3][4][1][2]
- **April 1986:** Groundbreaking of the DSM manufacturing facility in Normal, Illinois, bolstered by incentives from state and local governments.[1][2]
- **March 1988:** DSM plant completed and production begins with 240,000 annual vehicle capacity.[5][2][1]
- **1989:** Factory workers form United Auto Workers Local 2488 and sign the first labor contract with DSM.[1]
- **1991:** Facing financial challenges, Chrysler sells its 50% share of DSM to Mitsubishi. Mitsubishi assumes sole control and operation of the Normal, Illinois plant.[6][2][1]
- **1995:** DSM's corporate identity is retired, and the plant is officially renamed Mitsubishi Motor Manufacturing of America (MMMA).[2][1]
- **2002:** Name changes to Mitsubishi Motors North America, Manufacturing Division.[1]
- **2015:** Mitsubishi ends vehicle production at the Normal, Illinois plant, marking the final closure and end of the original DSM plant’s manufacturing operations.[7]

### Ownership and Structural Changes

- **1985-1991:** DSM operated as a joint 50:50 venture with shared design, engineering, and manufacturing, but facility management largely fell to Mitsubishi.[2]
- **1991 onward:** Mitsubishi becomes sole owner and operator of the DSM plant and vehicles, continuing Eclipse, Galant, and other lines on its own.[6][2][1]

***

These are the key structural and ownership changes in Diamond Star Motors' history, highlighting how it evolved from a collaborative effort to a wholly Mitsubishi-owned operation over three decades.[4][3][7][2][1]
