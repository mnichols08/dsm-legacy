The Mitsubishi 4G63 engine was developed as part of the "Sirius" (4G6x) engine family, which itself was inspired and preceded by several key Mitsubishi engine lines and models. Here’s a breakdown of those influences:

### Sirius Engine Family as the Direct Precursor

- The **Sirius engine family (4G6x)** debuted in the late 1970s and early 1980s, providing the architecture and technology that would directly give rise to the 4G63. Notable members before the 4G63 included the 4G61 (1.6L, DOHC and turbocharged in the Mirage/Lancer) and 4G67 (1.8L DOHC performance engine).[1][2][3]
- The 4G63’s closest ancestor is the **4G61** turbo and naturally aspirated engines, whose performance focus and multi-valve DOHC layout laid the foundation for the later 2.0L 4G63.[3][1]
- The first turbocharged Sirius, the **4G63T**, was introduced in the early 1980s, with notable appearances in the 1981 Mitsubishi Lancer EX 2000 Turbo and in Starion sports coupes, showing Mitsubishi’s dedication to high-performance four-cylinders well before the DSM era.[4][5][1]

### Other Foundational Mitsubishi Engine Families

- Before the Sirius series, Mitsubishi’s earlier engine families set important groundwork:
  - **Saturn (4G3x):** Early OHC aluminum-four designs (debuted in the late 1960s), establishing Mitsubishi’s inline-four platform.[6][2]
  - **Astron (4G5x):** Known for the “Silent Shaft” balancing system as seen in larger 2.6L turbo engines (popular in the Mitsubishi Starion), influencing durability and turbocharging strategies adopted for the Sirius engines.[2]
  - **Orion:** This series provided compact, efficient OHC designs for Mitsubishi’s small cars, but the technological leap was most evident in the Sirius and Astron lines.

### Early Performance Applications

- Performance inspiration came from turbocharged applications in the **1980s “Turbo Era”** like the Lancer EX 2000 Turbo and Mitsubishi Starion, where tuned four-cylinder engines were developed for both high performance and motorsport reliability.[5][1][4]
- These models’ engine and turbocharging technology directly informed the design of the 4G63, focusing on strength (iron block), rev capability, and forced induction readiness.[7][2]

***

In summary, the 4G63's development was primarily inspired by earlier Sirius family engines (notably the 4G61 and turbocharged Lancer/Starion units), while also drawing foundational lessons from the Saturn and Astron series for inline-four performance, reliability, and turbocharging technology.[1][2][4][7]
